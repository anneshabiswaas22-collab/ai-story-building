# Maple House English: first 10 uploads



All episodes: 20:00. Release order, not a claim that ten finished videos can be produced in ten working days. Build a production buffer before daily publishing.



Format: 24 cycles x (30 seconds story + 20 seconds recap). Core A1-A2; optional B1-B2 extensions are marked, not simultaneous four-level instruction.



## Day 1: Dad Mixed Up BOTH Shopping Lists! | Vegetable Market English | Slow Family Story



**Story:** Maya plans salad and Nina plans soup. Dan combines their color-coded shopping notes, misreads quantities and assigns the right vegetables to the wrong dishes. The children help him ask, check and sort. Everyone cooks together.

**Comedy:** Four versus forty carrots; silent bell pepper; corn with ears; a salad suspiciously resembling soup.

**Vocabulary:** carrot, tomato, cucumber, zucchini, potato, onion, broccoli, bell pepper, corn, lettuce, cabbage, spinach, basket, list, bag, receipt, change

**Grammar:** A1: this/these, singular/plural, numbers; A2: some, how many/how much, polite requests, simple past; B1 extension: clarification and because; B2 extension: respectful repair and if + present.

**Pronunciation:** American tomato, potato, vegetable; first-syllable stress in carrot/cucumber; clear final plural sounds.

**Arc:** Confidence -> confusion -> admission -> teamwork -> shared meal.

**Relative appearance:** None; establish all six permanent characters.



## Day 2: Dad Bought the Sourest Fruit! | Fruit Shop English | Funny Slow Story



**Story:** Dan wants perfect fruit for a family picnic. Max thinks sour means spoiled, and Dan chooses fruit by size alone. A tasting comparison and a budget challenge teach the family to describe what they want.

**Comedy:** A lemon is mistaken for a tiny yellow orange; the biggest fruit will not fit in the picnic basket.

**Vocabulary:** apple, banana, orange, lemon, lime, grape, strawberry, watermelon, peach, pear, ripe, sour, sweet, peel, slice, pound, bunch

**Grammar:** A1: colors and likes; A2: comparatives, a bunch of, too/enough; B1: preferences with because; B2: polite alternatives.

**Pronunciation:** grapes final /s/; ripe versus right; sentence stress in sweeter than.

**Arc:** Size contest -> sour surprise -> compare carefully -> fruit salad.

**Relative appearance:** None.



## Day 3: Flowers in the Pancakes?! | Kitchen English | Family Comedy



**Story:** Dan hears flour as flower while helping make breakfast. Maya catches the misunderstanding before anything is added. Nina turns the near-mistake into a kitchen scavenger hunt; the children measure correctly.

**Comedy:** An empty vase waits beside a mixing bowl; Dad asks whether pancakes need a bouquet.

**Vocabulary:** flour, flower, bowl, whisk, spoon, spatula, measuring cup, batter, pan, stove, plate, egg, milk, butter, pour, stir, flip

**Grammar:** A1: kitchen nouns and imperatives; A2: count/mass nouns, some/any, first/then; B1: before/after; B2: explaining a misunderstanding.

**Pronunciation:** flour/flower homophones; whisk initial /w/; pour versus poor varies by accent.

**Arc:** Misheard word -> check ingredients -> coordinated cooking -> imperfect but tasty breakfast.

**Relative appearance:** None; adults handle the hot stove.



## Day 4: Dad Tried On the Wrong Shoes! | Shopping Mall English | Slow Story



**Story:** The family shops for school shoes. Dan confuses his shoe box with Max's, then follows a map upside down. Maya's sister Rose appears briefly to help locate the fitting area, not to solve the whole problem.

**Comedy:** A giant adult foot next to a tiny shoe; Dad calls the escalator a moving staircase and keeps missing the sign.

**Vocabulary:** mall, entrance, elevator, escalator, store, size, pair, fitting room, shelf, cashier, receipt, refund, tight, loose, comfortable

**Grammar:** A1: this/that/these/those; A2: sizes, comparative adjectives, can I try; B1: explaining a problem; B2: polite returns.

**Pronunciation:** shoes /z/ ending; comfortable common American reduction; emphasis in too tight.

**Arc:** Wrong box -> wrong direction -> ask clearly -> find the right fit.

**Relative appearance:** Aunt Rose, Maya's sister: 60-90 seconds.



## Day 5: The Picnic Is on the WRONG Bench! | Park English | Family Adventure



**Story:** Two similar picnic spots create a harmless mix-up. Dan follows a drawing without checking its orientation. The children use landmarks to find Maya and Nina while keeping together.

**Comedy:** Every bench looks like the bench in Dad's drawing; Max tries to ask a statue for directions.

**Vocabulary:** park, garden, bench, fountain, path, pond, tree, flowerbed, gate, playground, blanket, picnic basket, shade, left, right, straight

**Grammar:** A1: where is and place words; A2: prepositions and directions; B1: relative clauses for identifying a bench; B2: checking an interpretation.

**Pronunciation:** left final cluster; between second-syllable stress; direction phrases with pauses.

**Arc:** Wrong landmark -> careful observation -> clear directions -> picnic reunion.

**Relative appearance:** No new relative.



## Day 6: Dad Said HOT… But Meant WARM! | Restaurant English | Funny Family Dinner



**Story:** At a neighborhood restaurant Dan uses hot ambiguously. The server clarifies temperature versus spicy flavor. The family practices ordering, changing an order politely and asking for the check.

**Comedy:** Dan rehearses a grand speech, then only needs: Could I have some water, please?

**Vocabulary:** menu, server, appetizer, main course, dessert, fork, knife, spoon, napkin, glass, mild, spicy, warm, order, check, takeout box

**Grammar:** A1: food names; A2: I'd like/could I have; B1: preferences and requests; B2: tactful correction without blame.

**Pronunciation:** would you connected speech; dessert stress versus desert; clear please.

**Arc:** Ambiguous order -> clarification -> enjoyable meal -> polite checkout.

**Relative appearance:** No new relative; server remains a minor supporting role.



## Day 7: Right Bus, Wrong Stop! | Travel English | Slow Family Comedy



**Story:** The family plans a library trip. Dan reads the departure time correctly but waits at the opposite stop. A quick call with Nina's father Ed helps them describe the direction, then they check the destination sign themselves.

**Comedy:** Dad proudly arrives early at the wrong side of the street; Max wonders whether a bus stop can move.

**Vocabulary:** bus stop, route, ticket, fare, driver, seat, timetable, destination, arrival, departure, library, block, intersection, umbrella

**Grammar:** A1: times and numbers; A2: present continuous and going to; B1: future plans and sequencing; B2: confirming travel details.

**Pronunciation:** thirteen/thirty stress; route accepted American variants; rising checks.

**Arc:** Good preparation -> wrong assumption -> verify destination -> reach the library.

**Relative appearance:** Grandpa Ed, Nina's father: 60-second phone cameo.



## Day 8: Grandma's Suitcase Has Everyone's Socks! | Home English | Family Story



**Story:** Maya's mother Ruth visits. Identical packing cubes mix up the family's laundry. Each person describes ownership and location; Grandma shares a gentle story about Dan's earlier packing mistake.

**Comedy:** Dad claims a tiny sock is a new travel-size fashion; Grandma produces a perfectly labeled checklist.

**Vocabulary:** suitcase, zipper, drawer, closet, hanger, towel, pillow, blanket, socks, pajamas, laundry, pocket, label, charger, unpack

**Grammar:** A1: household nouns; A2: possessives and whose; B1: simple past questions; B2: describing evidence and correcting an assumption.

**Pronunciation:** whose/who's homophones; drawers American rhotic ending; past -ed examples.

**Arc:** Warm welcome -> mixed belongings -> ownership game -> family memory.

**Relative appearance:** Grandma Ruth, Maya's mother: 90-120 seconds, then off-screen family visit.



## Day 9: Can Dad Grow Money? | Garden English | Funny Learning Story



**Story:** The family plants mint, basil and vegetables. Max hears mint and imagines coins; Dan misjudges how much water a pot needs. They read labels, measure water and make a growth journal.

**Comedy:** A pretend paper coin is used as a plant label, not planted; Dad gives a tiny seed a very large name.

**Vocabulary:** seed, soil, pot, root, stem, leaf, watering can, shovel, sunlight, shade, basil, mint, grow, dry, damp, label

**Grammar:** A1: plant parts; A2: needs and quantities; B1: if/when and future predictions; B2: explaining a process clearly.

**Pronunciation:** soil vowel; leaf/leaves consonant change; water common American flap.

**Arc:** Wordplay -> overconfident gardening -> measured care -> first shared observation.

**Relative appearance:** Uncle Ben, Dan's brother: a 60-second delivery of empty pots.



## Day 10: The Party Cake Went to the Wrong House! | Everyday English | Family Finale



**Story:** A smudged house number sends a cake delivery to the neighbor. The family retraces events using descriptions, directions and polite questions. The neighbor returns the cake, and the family celebrates their teamwork.

**Comedy:** Dan has perfect party decorations but no cake; Max volunteers a very small strawberry as the whole dessert.

**Vocabulary:** invitation, guest, neighbor, address, doorbell, balloons, ribbon, cake, candles, plate, decorations, delivery, mistake, explain, celebrate

**Grammar:** A1-A2 review; B1: past/present/future contrasts and because; B2: collaborative planning, polite disagreement and repair.

**Pronunciation:** address noun stress variants; clear house numbers; emphasis on the corrected word.

**Arc:** Party preparation -> missing cake -> reconstruct and ask -> warm payoff with callbacks.

**Relative appearance:** Brief returns of Rose and Ed; no large new cast.
