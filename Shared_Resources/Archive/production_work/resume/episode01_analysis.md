## Canonical format and identity

The film is 80 continuous story shots, 15 seconds each, exactly 00:00–20:00. Natural American-English vocabulary arises from practical decisions, jokes, misunderstandings and clarification. There are no learner-response slots, recap presenters, countdowns or quiz overlays. Optional creator-written practice belongs after the story. The illustrated package is preproduction, never proof of finished voices, animation or creator approval.

The permanent Brooks family has six members. Dan is father of all three children and husband of both Maya and Nina. Maya is Leo and Ella's mother; Nina is Max's mother. Maya and Nina have equal standing and mutual respect. Their family structure is an established fact, not a source of jealousy, ridicule or conflict. Relationships should remain consistent when introducing the later planned relatives.

## Story and emotional structure

1. **Shots 001–007: promise and misremembering.** Maya's BLUE salad note and Nina's YELLOW soup note are correctly labeled. Dan overlaps/folds them, confidently reverses their purposes in his memory, and dismisses Leo's offer to check. The children willingly join the errand.
2. **Shots 008–042: concrete shopping and accumulating evidence.** Dan and all three children visit the market while Maya/Nina stay home. The family chooses the right ingredients despite repeated verbal errors. Carrot counting, tomato protection, cucumber/zucchini comparison, potato purpose, uncut-onion joke, broccoli forest, silent bell pepper, corn ears and loose-leaf lettuce/cabbage give vocabulary a physical reason. Nothing magically talks or transforms.
3. **Shots 043–049: recognition, clarification and vulnerable admission.** The separate notes expose Dan's reversed dish names. Maya/Nina are phone voices from home; no unexplained appearance at the market. Dan asks for repetition, then admits he wanted to get everything right. Leo's response, “You can make a mistake and still be a good dad,” is the emotional center.
4. **Shots 050–061: visible cooperative repair and payment.** Blue and yellow totes are opened and filled from the one basket after explicit sorting ellipses. The family verifies total/payment/change/receipt and leaves together. The error is fixed through action, not a lecture.
5. **Shots 062–071: safe preparation and earned transformation.** Bags go to the correct adults. Hands/produce are washed, adults cut and cook, children help in safe areas, and an explicit time ellipsis prevents raw potatoes becoming soup instantly.
6. **Shots 072–080: warm meal and forward promise.** Fixed seating, two finished dishes, Dan's relief, both partners' appreciation, jokes recalling earlier misunderstandings, and the next day's one-list/two-color fruit plan. A teaser opens the next premise without leaving the current conflict unresolved.

The reusable dramatic principle is confidence → specific ambiguity → evidence → honest admission → shared correction → warm payoff. Reuse that principle, camera clarity and relational warmth, not the vegetable shopping plot or identical jokes. Dan should retain learning between episodes; later mistakes should arise from a new reasonable ambiguity, rather than erasing all progress. Each child and both women need independent, useful agency.

## Dialogue and pacing

Voices are natural General American. Exchanges are usually two or three short turns that motivate the visible action. Humor comes from concrete double meanings (“ears,” “bell,” “change”), scale errors (four/forty), and friendly callbacks. Imagined vegetable personalities remain jokes, never supernatural world rules. No humiliating labels, cruelty, jealousy or slapstick injury. Characters ask polite questions and model clarification naturally.

Retain breathing space for the emotional beat and physical action inside each 15-second slot. Dialogue-only SRT timings are provisional until actual performances. Do not imply that a 15-second scripted shot proves a provider can generate that duration or that recorded speech already exists.
