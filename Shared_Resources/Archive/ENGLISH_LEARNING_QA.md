# English-learning QA and optional post-story curriculum gaps

**Scope:** Review framework for the existing ten 20-minute American-English family-comedy stories. This document does **not** add exercises inside the films, claim a completed A1–B2 teaching program, or alter approved dialogue. It is a separate planning deliverable for Codex and the creator.

## Confirmed format

- All ten written packages have 80 × 15-second story slots with dialogue and corresponding provisional subtitles.
- Existing storylines teach object names and conversational expressions in context. A full graded A1/A2/B1/B2 skill map, lesson outcomes and optional post-story recorded practice segments are **not established** by the existing repository.
- Performed voice timing, pronunciation, General American delivery and listening comprehension can only be verified after audio exists.
- Don't add a teacher narrator, quizzes, timed response cards, 30-second recap breaks or other interruptions to the twenty-minute story without an explicit new owner decision.

## QA checklist for the existing story dialogue

1. Preserve natural General American expressions, not literal translations from other languages.
2. Avoid unsupported statements that a scripted speaker has already mastered a formal language level.
3. Preserve speaker identity and cast placement (especially off-screen adults, Grandpa Ed and minor supporting roles).
4. Keep conversational turn lengths plausible. Structural timing is not equivalent to a performed speech test.
5. Note likely hard vocabulary, context clues and natural reformulations for future optional support material.
6. Keep jokes family friendly. Both adult partners and all three children should have useful, respectful contributions.
7. Do not teach misinformation as a factual lesson (e.g., fictional shop/transport policies are not general facts).
8. Preserve one-word/listening misunderstandings as story events, but make the final clarification genuinely clear.
9. Use captions exactly aligned with accepted performances after audio is recorded; source SRT timings are drafts.
10. Separate language-learning success metrics (comprehension, speaking confidence) from video/image technical metrics.

## Missing optional post-story deliverables (not blockers for the existing stories)

- Creator-approved A1/A2/B1/B2 topic and vocabulary progression with measurable objectives.
- Optional short listening/speaking rehearsal scripts after the main story.
- American-English pronunciation guides and checked recorded voices.
- Visualized object vocabulary with consistent original scene references.
- A learner progress/assessment design outside the story runtime.
- Accessibility check of final captions once accepted audio timings exist.
