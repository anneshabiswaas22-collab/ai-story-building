## The empty vase and the two words

- Dan misinterprets **flour** as **flower**, bringing an **empty clear vase**, not a plant, toward the planning area. Maya checks what he means before any flower comes near food. No flower is picked or brought into the food-preparation area.
- The vase contains **no flower, water, flour, batter or utensil** at any time. It is returned to the original decoration shelf at **SHOT_010** and stays there. It never becomes a measuring jug or a second mixing bowl.
- **Flour and flower are homophones** in ordinary General American speech. Reading the package and recipe/context resolves the misunderstanding; do not claim that speaking one “more clearly” changes the word's pronunciation.
- Nina's kitchen search is a practical family task: find the right ingredients and tools to make breakfast. It is not an audience quiz, countdown, vocabulary recap or instruction to repeat after the characters.
- Use [FLOUR_FLOWER_LABELS.svg](assets/overlays/FLOUR_FLOWER_LABELS.svg) and the recipe artwork for exact written labels. Generated image lettering is not the authority.

## One recipe, one batch, twelve small pancakes

All ingredients come from the household kitchen. There is no shopping transaction or food budget in this episode. Package stock stays separate from the measured amount used in this single batch.

| Ingredient | Exact amount used | Destination |
|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour | **2 level cups** | Blue dry bowl |
| Sugar | **2 tablespoons** | Blue dry bowl |
| Baking powder | **1 tablespoon = 3 teaspoons** | Blue dry bowl |
| Salt | **1/2 teaspoon** | Blue dry bowl |
| Large eggs | **2** | Yellow wet bowl; adult cracks them |
| Milk | **1 1/2 cups** | Yellow wet bowl |
| Unsalted butter | **4 tablespoons total** | **3 tablespoons** melted/cooled into the wet mixture; **1 tablespoon** reserved for pan greasing across all six cooking batches |

The actual editorial references are [PANCAKE_RECIPE.svg](assets/overlays/PANCAKE_RECIPE.svg) and [COOKING_PLAN.svg](assets/overlays/COOKING_PLAN.svg). Do not substitute baking soda, confuse tablespoons with teaspoons, pack the flour, add an extra egg or treat the butter reserve as an additional four tablespoons.

- The **BLUE dry mixing bowl becomes the only batter bowl**. It receives the four measured dry ingredients, then the entire wet mixture.
- The **YELLOW wet bowl** holds the two eggs, milk and three tablespoons of cooled melted butter. After transfer into blue, it is empty and set aside; do not leave a duplicate wet mixture in it or refill it.
- Butter is split before melting. Only the three-tablespoon batter portion is melted and cooled for mixing. The one-tablespoon pan reserve stays separate and is used incrementally; it is not poured into the batter or served as extra table butter.
- Fold gently, accepting small lumps. One explicit **five-minute rest** is shown through a story-time ellipsis. No one eats raw flour, eggs or batter.
- A quarter-cup measure guides small equal portions; the adults divide the actual one-batch batter among twelve pancakes, adjusting the final scrape rather than creating a thirteenth pancake or a new bowlful.
- Cook **six batches of two**. The first pancake may be crooked, but is cooked through and kept. No air toss, burnt-food gag, discarded pancake or second batch is introduced.
- If an adult cuts the first pancake to check its center, its two halves are still **one pancake** and stay for Dan's serving. Never count the two pieces as two pancakes.

## Food, heat and final serving

Adults control raw eggs, butter heating, knives, stove knobs, hot pan and spatula. Children use cool ingredients/measures with supervision away from raw-egg splashes and heat. Wash hands before preparation and after raw-egg handling. Shells go to waste, never the batter; no raw taste is used to test the recipe. Keep the cooked tray and serving utensils clean and separate from the raw ladle/whisk/bowl.

Pancakes are cooked on both sides with a firm cooked center before serving; exterior color alone is not the proof. Keep the pan handle inward, use controlled low flips and show the burner being switched **OFF** after the last pair. Do not carry the hot pan to the children's table. Serve only the twelve completed small pancakes, two per person, in the unchanged seating plan.
