## One card, two colors; one basket and one cooler

- A **single shopping card** carries a **BLUE fruit half** and a **YELLOW picnic-supplies half**. - There is one **blue-handled woven basket** and one **insulated cooler**. They are distinct containers, never duplicated for a convenient camera angle. Keep cold packs and packed supplies separate from loose unwashed fruit. The script's opening/end states govern each transfer.
- At departure the basket contains **no fruit**. The cooler holds ice packs and sealed water bottles; plates, forks and napkins occupy its lid pocket, and the picnic blanket is rolled under its carry strap. Do not depict the cooler as entirely empty or the basket as already filled with purchases.
- The actual picnic blanket is **blue-and-white check**, as established when packed in SHOT_004. Preserve that fabric after unrolling it in the garden. Colors in the top-down seating diagram are schematic position markers.
- Shop display stock, free tasting samples, selected fruit and purchased fruit are four different inventory states. A fruit shown in a display remains shop stock until its scripted selection. A sample does not subtract from or add to the family's purchase quantities.

- Dan's wallet, card and receipt use his **RIGHT trouser pocket** when stowed. His watch stays on his **LEFT** wrist. Do not introduce a phone call or change these sides.

## Tasting and food handling

1. The shop's tasting portions are **free, fresh, already washed and prepared lemon/orange samples**, separate from the nine purchased fruit lines. All six wash their hands at the customer sink before tasting. Adults supervise the small portions and single-use utensils; no shared fork returns to a sample plate, and no handled sample returns to sale stock.
2. Max's worried “sour means spoiled” assumption is corrected. **A known fresh lemon is naturally sour; sourness alone does not establish spoilage.** Never have anyone taste unknown or suspect fruit to decide whether it is safe. Reject damaged or spoiled fruit without tasting it. Compare the known-safe lemon sample with the known-safe sweet orange sample.
3. Check fruit gently; ripeness is not judged by size alone and not every ripe fruit tastes sweet. No hard squeezing, throwing fruit, peel chewing or wasteful sampling. The giant melon is not opened for a joke.
4. At home everyone washes hands again. Wash produce under running water on clean surfaces before preparation; no soap on fruit. Adults handle knives, peel citrus/bananas, remove peach pits and apple/pear cores. Children take safe tasks with clean utensils, away from blades.
5. Adults **quarter grapes lengthwise for Max** before serving; do not show Max eating whole grapes. Keep pits, seeds and peel scraps out of the serving bowl and away from small mouths.
6. The salad uses the **2 apples, 4 bananas, 2 oranges, 1 pound of grapes, 1 pound of strawberries, 2 peaches and 2 pears**, plus juice from **HALF the purchased lemon and HALF the purchased lime**. The unused lemon and lime halves are wrapped and refrigerated. No extra fruit, sugar, dressing bottle or second lemon appears without a scripted source.
7. Preparation requires visible work or an explicit time ellipsis; raw whole fruit does not become a finished salad between two supposedly continuous frames. Keep covered fruit salad chilled in the cooler until the shaded backyard meal. Adults supervise serving; use individual portions and clean utensils.
