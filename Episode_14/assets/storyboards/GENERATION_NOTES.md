# Episode 14 storyboard sources

The four-panel sheets illustrate shots from [Can You Mail a Hug?](https://github.com/arunabanarjee32-bit/ai-story-building/blob/9ce74370da8e0799baf4528f7e7d5f872f62b46e/Episode_14/STORY.md). The [gallery](../../STORYBOARD_INDEX.md) links each panel to its timed screenplay shot.

## Image direction

Each PNG was generated for its four consecutive shots using the original adult and child lineups. The direction specified a warm, stylized 3D image, four equal landscape panels in reading order, the named actors and their W01 wardrobe. Each panel brief included the screenplay’s camera direction, action and continuity. The pictures are composition drafts for creator review.

The additional references for each sheet are listed below. Reference boards supplied a recurring prop or supporting character design. The screenplays establish each panel’s location, cast and prop state.

## Sheet 07 generation direction

The overhead insert shows seven creases and eight spaces between the two blue hand drawings. The lower panels follow the first accordion folds.

<details>
<summary>Image prompt for shots 025–028</summary>

> Use case: illustration-story. Use the first input, Episode 14 sheet 06, as the base for the next four panels. Replace all four pictures with shots 025–028 of the same paper-hug scene. Inputs two and three are the Brooks cast/wardrobe lineup. Fourth is the dining room; same honey oak table, wide garden-facing window, sunlight. The one important prop is ONE long, narrow strip of pale yellow paper with a blue outlined left palm on the FIRST panel and a blue outlined right palm on the EIGHTH and last. Exactly SEVEN straight, evenly spaced crosswise creases create EIGHT rectangles. Interior panels blank. No text, numbers, perforations, cut lines, writing or separate cards. Warm original stylized 3D family animation, gentle afternoon light. The deliverable is a WIDE landscape 16:9 sheet with four equal widescreen frames in a 2 by 2 arrangement separated by hairline ivory gutters.
>
> TOP LEFT, 025. Low oblique along the oak table to make the newly opened long single strip feel like a sidewalk. Leo (tallest child, orange shirt) smooths the last fold flat, Ella (coral shirt, full length denim overalls and two braids) pins the left end, and little Max (curly auburn hair, green T-shirt) sights along it with delighted curiosity. It has the same blue outline palms at the two extreme ends.
>
> TOP RIGHT, 026, the counting insert. Camera exactly overhead, long strip runs straight HORIZONTALLY across nearly the entire frame. Make it a clearly countable 1-by-8 row: one paper rectangle eight units wide and one unit tall, seven evenly spaced interior vertical folds with NO middle horizontal line. Place a BLUE OUTLINE palm on the far-left/first space and one on the far-right/eighth space. All six interior spaces completely blank. Show Nina's teal-cuffed hand indicating the FIRST space, Max’s small fingertip near the first palm and Ella’s two fingers walking the last space, but place hands at the edge so no crease is hidden. No faces needed in this overhead shot.
>
> BOTTOM LEFT, 027. Low end-on close at the left blue hand. Ella tucks the second rectangle UNDER the first one, leaving its blue palm visible outside. Max's single small fingertip pins the first palm, Leo's hand supports the long untouched length. Show two layers at this end and the still-long remainder of the SAME sheet. Their attentive faces in soft focus behind hands.
>
> BOTTOM RIGHT, 028. Side view across the tabletop. Max and Ella now reverse the next two folds. A small FOUR-segment zigzag at the near end (like the beginning of a paper fan in shallow profile) and the still-unfolded rest extending along the table. Dan in blue polo, mustache, plays along with a fingertip walking the air just below the paper steps; Leo in orange looks on. Preserve the uncut strip, single set of drawn hands, seven original creases. No paper staircase build, scissors, extra strip or duplicated props.

</details>
